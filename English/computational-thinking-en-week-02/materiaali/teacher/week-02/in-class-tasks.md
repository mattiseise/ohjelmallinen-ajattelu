# Week 2 — In-class tasks

## Overview

This file contains the **in-class, teacher-led** activities for Week 2 (Lessons 4, 5 and 6). They run during class, not as homework.

- **Activity A — "Dependency sort"** is **MANDATORY**. It belongs in the 60–80 min slot of Lesson 5 and is the centrepiece of that lesson's dependency work.
- **Activity B — "Two decompositions, one task"** is **OPTIONAL**. It fits the 55–75 min slot of Lesson 4 (everyday-task version) or Lesson 6 (IT-task version).

Both require live classroom interaction — cards on the board, pairs comparing decompositions, the teacher drawing arrows between subtasks. They cannot be replaced by homework or by an LLM.

---

## Activity A — "Dependency sort" 🟢 MANDATORY

**Goal.** Students see, in their own classroom, that dependencies are physical, visible objects — arrows between subtasks, not invisible rules in someone's head. The embodied act of sorting cards makes the difference between "depends on" and "free order" obvious in a way reading a list does not.

**Group size.** Whole class (one round on the board, then one round led by two student volunteers).

**Time required.** 20 min (Lesson 5, 60–80 min slot).

### Teacher preparation (before class)

1. Prepare **8 large cards** for one familiar everyday task. Suggested scenario: *organising a class trip*. Suggested subtasks (one per card):
   - *Collect parental consent forms.*
   - *Book the bus.*
   - *Pick the destination.*
   - *Set the date.*
   - *Email parents the itinerary.*
   - *Make a packing list.*
   - *Choose two teachers as chaperones.*
   - *Distribute the consent forms.*
2. Prepare a **second stack of 8 cards** for a different scenario, e.g. *sharing a file with the whole class* (find file, identify class group, set permissions, copy link, write email body, send email, confirm receipt, archive). This stack is for the volunteer-led second round.
3. Magnets, blu-tack or a magnetic whiteboard so cards can be moved and re-pinned.
4. A board marker for drawing dependency arrows.

### Step-by-step facilitation

1. **Set the rules (2 min).** *"You are going to sort these eight subtask cards into a valid order. A subtask depends on another when its input is the other subtask's output. When you spot a dependency, we draw an arrow between the cards."* Make clear that more than one valid order is possible — the class is looking for **a** defensible order, not **the** order.
2. **Round 1 — class sorts the trip cards (8 min).** Stick the 8 cards on the board in scrambled order. Call on students one at a time to move one card and justify the move with a dependency sentence: *"X depends on Y because …"*. Draw the arrow on the board as each dependency is named. Resist correcting silently; ask the class first.
3. **Naming free-order pairs (2 min).** Once the dependencies are drawn, ask: *"Which two cards have no arrow between them? Could they swap?"* Highlight at least one free pair.
4. **Round 2 — volunteer-led on a new scenario (6 min).** Hand the second stack to two student volunteers. They lead the sort in front of the class. The teacher stands at the back and only intervenes if the class gets stuck for over a minute.
5. **Close (2 min).** Each student takes the back of their L4 decomposition (Task 2.1) and quickly sketches dependency arrows between their own subtasks. The teacher walks around and checks two or three — looking specifically for at least one named dependency and at least one "free" pair.

### Debrief questions (use 1–2 at the close or in Lesson 6's warm-up)

- Which subtask had the most dependencies pointing at it? Why?
- Were there any subtasks the class first thought were dependent but turned out to be free?
- Which subtask in your own decomposition got the most arrows? Was it the right size, or is it actually two subtasks?

### Why it requires the classroom

The embodied, public act of sorting and re-pinning cards makes dependencies tangible. Watching two volunteers lead the second round also shifts ownership — the class teaches itself. Neither effect survives in homework.

### Cross-reference

This activity occupies the **60–80 min slot of Lesson 5** in `teacher-materials.md`. The teacher prepares both card stacks before that lesson.

## Activity B — "Two decompositions, one task" 🟣 OPTIONAL

**Goal.** Students notice that more than one valid decomposition can exist for the same task. Pair A and Pair B independently decompose the **same** task and then compare boundaries and sub-goals. Plants the seed for Week 4 ("comparing valid solutions").

**Group size.** Pairs. (Odd numbers: one group of three.)

**Time required.** 20 min (Lesson 4, 55–75 min slot, on an everyday task; or Lesson 6, 55–75 min slot, on the same IT scenario).

### Teacher preparation (before class)

- One named everyday task for the L4 version (e.g. *make breakfast for four people*) or one named IT scenario for the L6 version (e.g. *set up a new colleague's email on a new laptop*). One sentence on the board — no further detail.
- Paper and pens; no slides during the activity.

### Step-by-step facilitation

1. **Explain the rules (2 min).** Both pairs (A and B) decompose the **same** task into 4–6 subtasks with sub-goals. They work independently, without comparing notes, for 8 minutes.
2. **Independent decomposition (8 min).** Pairs work on paper. Silent. The teacher does not coach.
3. **Swap and compare (6 min).** Pairs swap papers. Each pair looks at the other's decomposition and writes down: (a) same number of subtasks? (b) same boundaries between subtasks? (c) where sub-goals differ. They identify one merge or split they might borrow.
4. **Whole-class debrief (4 min).** Two pairs share. The teacher writes the headline message on the board: *"More than one valid decomposition can exist for the same task — we will return to choosing between them in Week 4."*

### Debrief questions

- Was your decomposition close to your neighbour's, or surprisingly different?
- Where did the boundaries shift? Did one pair merge two subtasks the other split?
- Which decomposition would be easier to hand to a literal follower? Why?

### Cross-reference

This activity can sit in **Lesson 4 (55–75 min)** on an everyday task, or in **Lesson 6 (55–75 min)** as the pair-review step before AT1 submission. `teacher-materials.md` references both possibilities.
