# Week 2 — Decomposing problems

## Week 2 theme

This week introduces a new move: **decomposition**. When a task is too big to fit in one flat list of steps, you name the **main task** with its goal, split it into a handful of **subtasks** that each have their own small goal, and then think carefully about the **order** in which those subtasks have to happen. Lesson 4 names the move and the vocabulary. Lesson 5 sharpens the move by introducing dependencies (what depends on what). Lesson 6 puts everything to work on a realistic IT task, and the week closes with **AT1**, your first assessed task, submitted at the end of Lesson 6.

## What you will be able to do by the end of the week

- Define *decomposition*, *main task*, *subtask*, *sub-goal* and *dependency* in plain English.
- Look at one of your own everyday tasks and split it into a main task plus 4–6 subtasks with named sub-goals.
- Look at one realistic IT task and do the same — split, name, order.
- Put a list of subtasks in a defensible order and point to at least one dependency between them.
- Spot subtasks that have no dependency on each other and explain why their order is free.
- Submit AT1: a short artefact that decomposes one task and calls out at least one dependency.

## Core minimum vs. deepening

**Must-do minimum — every student should leave Week 2 able to:**
- Define the five core terms above in plain English.
- Decompose one everyday task and one IT task into a main task + 4–6 subtasks with named sub-goals.
- Put the subtasks in a defensible order and call out at least one dependency.
- Submit AT1: a short artefact that does the above for one task of your choice.

**Deepening (optional, not required for the assessed task):**
- Spot a subtask that is itself big enough to be split further.
- Find subtasks with no dependency on each other and describe where the order is free.
- Compare two valid decompositions of the same task and say which one is easier for a literal follower.
- Use an LLM as a sparring partner against your own decomposition, with a structured prompt that asks it to challenge rather than answer.

## How the three lessons fit together

The three lessons form one arc. **Lesson 4** names the new move: a big task becomes a main task with a goal and a handful of subtasks, each with its own sub-goal. **Lesson 5** sharpens that move by asking which subtasks **depend** on which — some have to come in a fixed order because their input is another subtask's output, and others are free. **Lesson 6** stress-tests the whole move on a realistic IT scenario and ends with AT1. Name the move, sharpen the move, use the move on something real.
