# Week 3 — Drawing the process

## Week 3 theme

Week 2 ended with **decomposition** — main task, subtasks, sub-goals — and you submitted **AT1** at the start of this week. Week 3 asks: *how do I write a process down so someone else can run it?* The three lessons cover the **simple flowchart** (L7), **abstraction** (L8), and **the informal idea of a function without code** (L9).

## What you will be able to do by the end of the week

- State the Week 3 central message in your own words.
- Name the four flowchart shapes — oval (start/end), rectangle (step), diamond (decision), arrow — and read a small flowchart.
- Draw a small flowchart (up to 8 shapes) for an everyday process with at least one decision point.
- Define *flowchart*, *decision*, *abstraction*, *level of detail*, *reusable block / function (Week 3 sense)*.
- Name at least two details hidden inside a familiar abstraction such as a "Login" button.
- Spot a chunk of process that appears in two processes and propose a short verb-phrase name for it.

## Must-do minimum vs. deepening

**Must-do minimum — every student should leave Week 3 able to:**

- Define the core terms above in plain English.
- Read a small flowchart and describe what it does and where each path ends.
- Draw a small flowchart for an everyday process from your own life, with at least one decision point.
- Name at least two hidden details inside one familiar abstraction.
- Spot one reused chunk across two flowcharts and propose a short name for it.

**Deepening (optional):**

- Rewrite one step as a lower-level mini-flowchart of 3–5 sub-steps, naming the audience.
- Find three reusable blocks across two or more processes and name each.
- Use an LLM as a sparring partner to challenge your abstraction choices, with an accept / modify / reject step.

## How the three lessons fit together

**Lesson 7** gives a process its first picture — the simple flowchart. **Lesson 8** asks which boxes deserve to be drawn and which can be hidden — that is abstraction. **Lesson 9** spots the same chunk reappearing across flowcharts and gives it a reusable name. Week 3 has no AT submission, but this week's vocabulary is exactly what **AT2 — process description** expects at the end of Week 5 / during Week 6.
