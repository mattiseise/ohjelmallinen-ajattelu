# Week 8 — Closing the loop

## Week 8 theme

The closing week. Nothing new conceptually — everything since Week 1 finally meets in one small file you can read top to bottom and defend out loud. L22 adds **loops in code** in JS and C# side by side; L23 is the build day; L24 is test, fix one bug, present. **AT3** — your first real program plus a **2-minute** oral presentation — is submitted on L24.

## What you will be able to do by the end of the week

- Read a small `for` or `while` loop and predict what it prints.
- Name the four parts of a `for` loop: counter, condition, body, update.
- Translate one piece of your Week 6 pseudocode into a 10–25-line JS or C# program.
- Run it in the browser console (JS) or .NET Fiddle (C#) and capture the output.
- Read a real error message, find the line, apply one small fix.
- Give a **2-minute** oral presentation: what, how, what went wrong, how you fixed it.

## Must-do minimum vs. deepening

**Must-do minimum — every student should leave Week 8 able to:**

- Read a short `for` loop and say what it will print.
- Translate one piece of Week 6 pseudocode into a working JS or C# program.
- Run with two different starting values; screenshot the output.
- Trigger one error, read the message, fix one line, re-run.
- Deliver the **2-minute** oral presentation on L24.

**Deepening (optional, not required for AT3 pass):**

- Combine a loop with an `if/else`.
- Compare with a classmate's other-language version in 3–5 sentences.
- Add input from `prompt()` (JS) or `Console.ReadLine()` (C#).

## How the three lessons fit together

L22 adds the last missing concept — the loop — so sequence, condition, repetition, variables, input and output are all available in code. L23 is the build day. L24 treats your own program like a stranger's: test, read, fix, present in 2 minutes. Loops → build → test, fix, present.

## Choosing your language (reminder from Week 7)

You picked JS or C# at the end of Week 7 and stay with it for AT3. Every example in `self-study.md` and on the slides appears in both languages side by side; tasks live in `student-tasks-js.md` and `student-tasks-csharp.md`. JS runs in the browser DevTools console; C# in **.NET Fiddle**. No installation.
