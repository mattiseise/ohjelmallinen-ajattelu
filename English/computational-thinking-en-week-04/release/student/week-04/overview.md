# Week 4 — Comparing solutions

## Week 4 theme

Week 4 is the **mid-course reflection week**. You turn a critical eye on the artefacts you built in Weeks 1–3. **L10** shows one problem can have more than one correct solution. **L11** names four **criteria** — *clarity*, *efficiency*, *repeatability*, *error-proneness*. **L12** uses them in a structured **peer-review and revision** protocol. The central message, verbatim:

> **More than one valid solution can solve the same problem. Picking between them is not a matter of taste — it is a matter of stated criteria.**

## What you will be able to do by the end of the week

- Recognize that one problem can have more than one correct solution.
- Give one everyday and one IT example of two valid solutions.
- Name and define the four criteria in your own words.
- Compare two short solutions criterion-by-criterion.
- Give a partner two pieces of criterion-named feedback pointing to a specific step.
- Revise one of your own Week 2 or Week 3 solutions and explain what changed and why.

## Must-do minimum vs. deepening

**Must-do minimum — every student should leave Week 4 able to:**

- State the central message in their own words.
- Name the four criteria with a one-sentence definition each.
- Show one everyday and one IT example of two valid solutions.
- Compare two short, given solutions criterion-by-criterion, in writing.
- Run the peer-review protocol on a partner's Week 2 or Week 3 artefact and produce two criterion-named pieces of feedback.
- Revise one of their own artefacts with a 3–5 sentence change note.

**Deepening (optional):**

- Apply the four criteria to a vocational scenario — backup, onboarding, or a help-desk macro.
- Describe a **trade-off**: one solution wins on clarity, the other on efficiency.
- Use an LLM as a sparring partner with an accept / modify / reject step.
- Run a second revision pass 24 hours later.

## How the three lessons fit together

**L10** shows the same problem can be solved two correct ways. **L11** gives the differences a vocabulary — four criteria, so *"I like this one"* becomes *"this one is clearer at step 3"*. **L12** uses them in a peer-review-and-revision cycle. Week 4 has no AT submission, but the habit is exactly what **AT2 (the Week 5/6 process description)** will ask you to do.
