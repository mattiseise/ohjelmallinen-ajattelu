# Week 4 — In-class tasks

## Overview

This file contains the **in-class, teacher-led** activities for Week 4.

- **Activity A — "Peer review with the four criteria"** is **🟢 MANDATORY**, slotted in the **22–52 min of Lesson 12**. It is the centrepiece of Week 4 — the criteria → feedback → revision protocol that AT2 (Week 5/6) will ask students to repeat on their own work.
- **Activity B — "Variation hunt"** is **🟣 OPTIONAL**, slotted in the **55–75 min of Lesson 10**. It is the lead-in to the L11 criteria.

Both need live classroom interaction — the silent read, the no-defending Q&A, and the on-the-spot bouncing of *taste* back to *criterion* all depend on the room. They cannot be replaced by homework or by an LLM.

## Activity A — "Peer review with the four criteria" 🟢 MANDATORY

**Goal.** Students run the full three-step peer-review protocol — silent read, criterion-named sentences, pick two, exchange with no defending — on a real partner's real artefact from Week 2 or Week 3. The revision happens in the L12 independent block that follows.

**Group size.** Pairs. Odd numbers: the isolated student pairs with **the teacher**, not with another isolated student.

**Time required.** 30 min (Lesson 12, 22–52 min slot).

### Teacher preparation (before class)

1. **Pre-write the three-step protocol on the board** in big letters: *(1) Silent read — 4 min. (2) Criterion pass — 10 min — one sentence per criterion in fixed order. (3) Pick two — 3 min — circle the two most useful, hand them over. Clarifying questions only, no defending.*
2. **Print 4–6 copies of the standby artefact pair** from `student-tasks.md`. Hand a standby to anyone empty-handed at the L12 warm-up (0:00–0:10).
3. **Pair the room if you can** — students with **very different artefacts** is the strongest pairing.
4. Board marker, fresh paper, a visible timer.

### Step-by-step facilitation

1. **Set the rules (2 min).** *"Silent read means silent. Four sentences in fixed order. Pick two. Clarifying questions allowed; defending is not."*
2. **Silent read (4 min).** Reviewer reads the partner's artefact once, all the way through. The author does not narrate or point.
3. **Criterion pass (10 min).** Reviewer writes one sentence per criterion in fixed order: *"Strong on X because…"* or *"Could be improved on X because…"*. Each names the criterion and points to a **specific step or block**. The teacher rotates and bounces *"I just like…"* back to *"on which criterion?"*.
4. **Pick two (3 min).** Reviewer circles the two most useful sentences — *"most useful to your partner in the next twenty minutes, not the cleverest two."*
5. **Exchange and 5-min clarifying Q&A (about 10 min for both directions).** Hand over the two pieces. The author may ask **one clarifying question per piece**. **No defending.** Swap roles and repeat.
6. **Hand back to the author.** The revision step lives in the **52–82 min INDEPENDENT WORK block** of L12.

### Teacher's job during the 30 minutes

Rotate. Listen. Bounce. Three things to listen for, in order:

- *"I just like this one better"* → *"on which criterion?"*
- *"This is wrong"* → *"on which criterion is it weak?"*
- *"3/5 on clarity"* → *"in words, not numbers — what specifically?"*

The teacher's role is **not** to evaluate the artefacts. It is to police *taste → criterion* in real time.

### Debrief questions (for the 1:22–1:28 whole-class slot of L12)

- Which criterion was hardest to write a sentence for, and why?
- Did anyone get feedback that pointed to a step they had not noticed was weak?
- Did any pair find that two of the four criterion sentences contradicted each other? (Trade-off in the wild.)

### Why it requires the classroom

The silent read, the no-defending Q&A, and the on-the-spot bouncing of *taste* back to *criteria* are the heart of the activity. None survive in homework or in a chat with an LLM — an LLM will simulate criterion-named feedback, but it cannot run the four-minute silence or refuse to defend. The training effect is in the room.

### Cross-reference

Slot: **22–52 min of Lesson 12** in `teacher-materials.md`. The revision step (52–82 min INDEPENDENT WORK) is also in the L12 plan. Standby artefact pair lives at the end of `student-tasks.md`.

## Activity B — "Variation hunt" 🟣 OPTIONAL

**Goal.** Pairs see, with their own eyes, that two valid solutions to the same problem can differ in more **kinds of ways** than *"the steps are in a different order"*. This is the **seed of the L11 criteria** — the *kinds of difference* the class names today will be re-cast as *clarity*, *efficiency*, *repeatability*, *error-proneness* tomorrow.

**Group size.** Pairs. Odd numbers: one group of three. **Time required.** 20 min (Lesson 10, 55–75 min slot).

### Teacher preparation (before class)

Each student needs their two L10 alternative algorithms from the 0:25–0:55 independent block. Clear the right-hand side of the board.

### Step-by-step facilitation

1. **Explain the rules (2 min).** *"Pairs swap two-algorithm pairs. Each of you writes at least two **kinds of ways** your partner's two solutions differ. Plain English — no jargon yet."*
2. **Independent read and write (8 min).** Pair A reads Pair B's two algorithms and writes at least two ways they differ. Pair B does the same. The teacher does not coach — silence is the point.
3. **Pair share (5 min).** Pairs exchange the differences they noticed. The author may not defend — only listen.
4. **Whole-class harvest (4 min).** Collect 4–5 *kinds of difference* on the board. Likely catches: *"different step order"*, *"one leaves something to the user, the other does not"*, *"one has more steps"*, *"one is easier to forget a step in"*, *"one always gives the same result; the other depends on conditions"*.
5. **Closing one-liner (1 min).** *"Each of those **kinds of difference** is going to get a name tomorrow. Four names."* Do **not** name the four criteria yet.

### Debrief questions

- Which kind of difference came up most often? Which was rarest?
- Did any pair find a kind that did not fit any obvious category?

### Why it requires the classroom

The simultaneous, visible difference between many pairs hunting the same kinds of difference is what makes the L11 criterion lecture land the next day. With the variation hunt, the four criteria arrive as **names for things the class has already noticed**.

### Cross-reference

Slot: **55–75 min of Lesson 10** in `teacher-materials.md`. The board harvest is the lead-in to L11.
