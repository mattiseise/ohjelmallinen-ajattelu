# Week 4 — Student tasks

## Overview of the week's tasks

This week has **four tasks**. The 🟢 MANDATORY one is the **must-do minimum** — Task 4.1, where you run the peer-review-and-revision protocol on one of your own Week 2 or Week 3 artefacts. The three 🟣 DEEPENING tasks are optional vocational extensions.

**AT2 — process description — is submitted at the end of Week 5 / during Week 6.** AT2 asks you to *revise your own work after feedback* — exactly this week's habit. Keep every peer-feedback note, every change note, and every before-and-after pair.

The four criteria — **clarity · efficiency · repeatability · error-proneness** — are **descriptive lenses**, not a scoring rubric. *"Recipe A is clearer at step 5 because three minutes is precise"* is what we want. *"4/5 on clarity"* is not.

---

## Task 4.1 — Peer review with the four criteria 🟢 MANDATORY

**Goal.** Bring a Week 2 decomposition or Week 3 algorithm/flowchart to class, run the three-step peer-review protocol on a partner's artefact, and revise your own in response. This is the **must-do minimum** for Week 4.

### Steps

1. **Bring your own artefact** — a Week 2 decomposition, your Week 3 Task 3.1 flowchart, or your Week 3 Task 3.4 redraw. If you have none, use the **standby artefact pair** at the end of this file.
2. **Silent-read (4 min).** The reviewer reads the partner's artefact once, all the way through, without speaking. The author does not narrate.
3. **Criterion pass — four sentences (10 min).** In the fixed order, write one sentence per criterion. Each is either *"Strong on X because…"* or *"Could be improved on X because…"*, naming the criterion and a **specific step or block**:
    - **Clarity** — *"…unclear at step 3 because the word **file** does not say which file."*
    - **Efficiency** — *"…efficient because step 2 and step 4 do not repeat each other."*
    - **Repeatability** — *"…could be improved on repeatability because step 5 says **wait a bit**."*
    - **Error-proneness** — *"…strong on error-proneness because the diamond catches the **no** case before any data is deleted."*
4. **Pick two (3 min).** Circle the **two most useful** sentences. Two is the limit.
5. **Exchange and 5-minute clarifying Q&A.** Hand over your two sentences. The author may ask **one clarifying question per piece**. **No defending.** Only clarifying. Swap roles and repeat.
6. **Revise your own artefact (about 25 min).** Take **one** of the two pieces of feedback and act on it. The revision does not have to be big — a five-word change can be the point.
7. **Write a 3–5 sentence change note.** What changed? Which criterion drove the change? Why that piece and not the other? Name the criterion **by name**.

### Example change note

> "I changed step 5 of my Week 3 *umbrella* flowchart. The original said *'check the weather'*; my partner pointed out (clarity) that this does not say **which app, which forecast, which time horizon**. I replaced step 5 with three smaller steps. I did not act on the efficiency feedback — for a first-time follower, separating the steps is safer (clarity/efficiency trade-off)."

### If you are doing this alone

If pair-work cannot happen, you become both reviewer and author, with **time** as the substitute for someone else's eyes. Do **one**:

- **24-hour-gap self-review.** Put the artefact away for at least 24 hours. The next day, run the silent-read + four-criterion-sentences + pick-two pass **as if a colleague had handed it to you**.
- **Class chat or course platform.** Post a photo and ask: *"One criterion-named piece of feedback by end of day, please."* One reply is enough.
- **A different device or a printout.** Read the artefact on a different device or as a printout. The change of medium catches some of what a partner would.

The point is to read your own work *with someone else's eyes* — including your own, 24 hours older.

### Keep your notes

Keep the two pieces of feedback, the before artefact, the after artefact, and the 3–5 sentence change note. **AT2 — process description — is submitted at the end of Week 5 / during Week 6**, and asks you to describe a process **and revise it after feedback** — exactly this loop.

## Task 4.3 — Backup methods, criterion by criterion 🟣 DEEPENING

**Goal.** Take a real backup scenario, write two valid solutions, compare with the four criteria, name the trade-off (vocational).

### Steps

1. **Pick a real backup scenario** (school project folder; phone before an update).
2. **Write Method A as a 5–8 step algorithm.** Suggestion: *USB copy*.
3. **Write Method B as a 5–8 step algorithm.** Suggestion: *zip + email to self*, or *OneDrive sync*.
4. **Fill in the four-criterion table** — one short sentence per cell. Describe; do not rank.

    | Criterion | Method A | Method B |
    |---|---|---|
    | Clarity | | |
    | Efficiency | | |
    | Repeatability | | |
    | Error-proneness | | |

5. **Trade-off paragraph (3–5 sentences).** *"In which situation would I pick A? In which would I pick B? Which criterion is deciding?"* Name the **situation** that flips the decision.

> **Tip.** *USB copy* often wins on clarity but loses on repeatability. *OneDrive sync* wins on repeatability but loses on clarity.

### Keep your notes

Keep both algorithms, the table, and the trade-off paragraph. **AT2 — process description — is submitted at the end of Week 5 / during Week 6**, and this is exactly the artefact AT2 will reward.

## Task 4.4 — Help-desk macro: long vs. short 🟣 DEEPENING

**Goal.** Write two help-desk responses to the same ticket — one long, one short with a checklist. Compare with the four criteria.

### The ticket

> *"Hi, I cannot log in to the school portal on my new phone. It worked on my old phone yesterday. The portal opens but my password does not work. — A."*

### Steps

1. **Write Macro A — one long step-by-step reply.** 6–10 ordered steps from symptom to *"if it still does not work, reply to this ticket"*.
2. **Write Macro B — short reply plus a checklist.** A short opening (2–3 sentences), then a *"If it is still not working, try X / Y / Z"* checklist of 3–4 items.
3. **Fill in the four-criterion table.**

    | Criterion | Macro A (long) | Macro B (short + checklist) |
    |---|---|---|
    | Clarity | | |
    | Efficiency | | |
    | Repeatability | | |
    | Error-proneness | | |

4. **Reflection (3–5 sentences).** Which would you send as a first-line agent on a busy Monday morning? Which to a user who has already replied twice? Which criterion drives the choice each time?

5. **Optional sparring-partner step.** Paste both macros and your table into an LLM with the prompt below. **Decide accept / modify / reject** for each challenge.

    ```
    You are my sparring partner, not my teacher. I am a vocational ICT
    student practising solution comparison. Below are two help-desk
    responses plus my four-criterion comparison.

    Ticket: [paste]
    Macro A (long): [paste]
    Macro B (short + checklist): [paste]
    My comparison table: [paste]

    Challenge my criterion analysis. Where does my "clarity" sentence
    not match the artefact? Where am I confusing efficiency with
    brevity? Where might error-proneness pull the opposite way?

    Do not rewrite the responses. Ask me a question that helps me see
    the gap in my own analysis.
    ```

    **Do not paste the LLM's text into your final table.** If you accept more than 80% of its challenges, you are not pushing back hard enough.

### If you are doing this alone

This task is **already solo by design**. The sparring step is also solo. For a second human eye, post your comparison table in the class chat: *"Did I miss anything on error-proneness?"*

### Keep your notes

Keep both macros, the table, the reflection, and — if you ran it — the LLM exchange with your decisions. **AT2 — process description — is submitted at the end of Week 5 / during Week 6**, and a process at two levels of granularity is strong AT2 raw material.

## Task 4.5 — Onboarding a new user: account-first vs. tour-first 🟣 DEEPENING

**Goal.** Write two short onboarding flows for the same new user. Compare with the four criteria. Name the **deciding criterion** and why it depends on the situation.

### Scenario

A new first-year student joins the school portal. They have a paper letter with their username and a temporary password. As the IT helper, you have ten minutes.

### Steps

1. **Write Flow A — account-first (5–8 steps).** Create the account / log in / change the password / **then** offer a tour.
2. **Write Flow B — tour-first (5–8 steps).** Tour the menu first / **then** create the account.
3. **Fill in the four-criterion table.**

    | Criterion | Flow A (account-first) | Flow B (tour-first) |
    |---|---|---|
    | Clarity | | |
    | Efficiency | | |
    | Repeatability | | |
    | Error-proneness | | |

4. **Trade-off paragraph (3–5 sentences).** Name the **deciding criterion** for *this* situation, and one short reason **why it would change** in a different one. Example: *"If the temporary password expires after one hour, Flow A wins on error-proneness. If it is good for a week, error-proneness is no longer deciding."*

### If you are doing this alone

This task is **already solo by design**. For a second eye, post the two flows in the class chat: *"Which would you pick for an anxious first-year — on which criterion?"*

### Keep your notes

Keep both flows, the table, and the trade-off paragraph. **AT2 — process description — is submitted at the end of Week 5 / during Week 6**, and an artefact showing *the same goal, two valid flows, and a named trade-off* is among the strongest AT2 building blocks.

## Standby artefacts (use only if you do not have your own Week 2 or Week 3 work)

Pick **one** as your "own" artefact for Task 4.1. Both contain **deliberate weaknesses** across the four criteria. Tell your partner you are using a standby; do not tell them where the weaknesses are.

### Standby A — A 4-step decomposition (Week 2 style)

**Main task.** *Hand in a written school assignment by Friday 23:59.*

**Subtasks:** (1) Write the assignment in a document. (2) Save the file. (3) Submit it on the portal. (4) Check that it went through. *(Dependencies: 2→1; 3→2; 4→3.)*

### Standby B — A 6-step algorithm (Week 3 style)

**Goal.** *Back up the school project folder before reformatting the laptop.*

(1) Find a USB drive. (2) Plug it in. (3) Copy the project files. (4) Wait for the copy. (5) Pull out the USB drive. (6) Reformat the laptop.
