# Lesson 11 — Criteria walkthrough

A side-by-side walkthrough of the four criteria on the two tea recipes.

Use during the L11 lecture block (10–35 min). Skip if the whiteboard sketch lands well.

---

## Slide 1 — Worked example: two tea recipes

Two recipes. Same goal: **a drinkable cup of tea**.

Both are *valid solutions*. They differ in step order and in what the follower has to judge.

The point of this walkthrough is not to crown a winner. It is to look at both through the four criteria, one criterion at a time.

---

## Slide 2 — Recipe A (boil-first)

1. Fill the kettle with 250 ml of water.
2. Boil the water.
3. Put one tea bag into the cup.
4. Pour the boiled water over the bag.
5. Wait 3 minutes, then remove the bag.

Five steps. Boil first, then bag, then pour, then a precise 3-minute wait.

---

## Slide 3 — Recipe B (steep-first)

1. Put one tea bag into the cup.
2. Fill the kettle with 250 ml of water.
3. Boil the water.
4. Pour the boiled water over the bag.
5. Wait until the colour looks right, then remove the bag.

Five steps. Bag first, then boil, then pour, then a colour-judged wait.

Same five-step shape. The order of steps 1–3 differs, and step 5 ends with a judgement, not a clock.

---

## Slide 4 — Both reach the goal — but the steps differ

- **Step order**: A boils before placing the bag; B places the bag before boiling.
- **Stopping cue**: A uses a clock (*3 minutes*); B uses the eye (*the colour looks right*).
- **What is left to the follower**: A leaves almost nothing — anyone with a kettle can do it. B leaves *what the right colour looks like* to the follower's experience.
- **Number of vessels touched**: identical. Number of steps: identical.

Both reach a drinkable cup. The differences are *real*, but they are not *errors*.

---

## Slide 5 — The four criteria, side by side

| Criterion | Recipe A (boil-first) | Recipe B (steep-first) |
|---|---|---|
| **Clarity** | Step 5 names a number (3 minutes) — a literal follower stops at the right time. | Step 5 names a colour the follower has to know — a new follower may stop too early or too late. |
| **Efficiency** | One boil, one steep, one cup of tea — no wasted steps. | One boil, one steep, one cup of tea — also no wasted steps. |
| **Repeatability** | Same kettle, same time → same drink on Monday and Friday. | Same kettle, same colour-judgement → drink depends on the day's eye. |
| **Error-proneness** | Hard to do wrong if the follower has a watch. | Risk of pulling the bag too early or too late if the follower is new. |

One sentence per cell. No numbers. No grade.

---

## Slide 6 — The trade-off in plain English

A is **clearer** (a number beats a colour for a literal follower).

A is **more repeatable** (a clock does not have a tired Friday).

B is **just as efficient** (same number of steps, same boil).

B can be **less error-prone for the practised tea-drinker** — they read the colour faster than they can set a timer, and they correct on the fly.

The "best" choice depends on the follower. New follower → A. Practised follower → either. **Naming the trade-off is the analysis.** No winner is required.
