# Week 6 — From thinking to code

## Week 6 theme

This week the plain-English thinking of Weeks 1–5 folds into a single, more precise notation: **pseudocode**. The central message is that pseudocode is the bridge between everyday English and real code — precise enough for a computer to follow if it had to, readable enough to share with a non-programmer. Lesson 16 introduces pseudocode and a small keyword set. Lesson 17 names the input → processing → output skeleton and the *variable*. Lesson 18 trains reading pseudocode line by line on paper with a *trace table*. **AT2 is submitted around Lesson 18**, and L16–L18 is exactly its raw material.

## What you will be able to do by the end of the week

- Define *pseudocode* and say how it differs from everyday English.
- Read a short piece of pseudocode and say what it does.
- Write 10–20 lines of pseudocode using `SET`, `IF/ELSE/ENDIF` and at least one of `WHILE/ENDWHILE` or `FOR/ENDFOR`.
- Identify the input, processing and output sections inside pseudocode.
- Define *variable* and use a name that describes what it holds.
- Dry-run pseudocode on paper with a trace table.
- Find and fix a bug by dry-running.

## Must-do minimum vs. deepening

**Must-do minimum — every student should leave Week 6 able to:**

- Define *pseudocode*, *variable*, *dry run*, *trace table* and *bug* in plain English.
- Write 10–20 lines of pseudocode for a familiar process you personally do, with at least one condition AND at least one repetition.
- Dry-run it on a trace table for one normal input and one awkward input.

**Deepening (optional, but recommended because AT2 lands this week):**

- Find a bug in a broken piece of pseudocode and name its type in plain English.
- Trace a classmate's pseudocode without first asking what it does.
- Use an LLM as a sparring partner against your own trace.

## How the three lessons fit together

L16 introduces pseudocode as a tighter shape for Week 5's sequence, condition and repetition. L17 zooms in on the *variable* inside an input → processing → output skeleton. L18 trains dry-running on paper. The arc — write it, name its parts, prove it works — is exactly the arc AT2 asks for.
