# Week 5 — Three moves: sequence, condition, repetition

## Week 5 theme

**Almost any process can be described with three building blocks: sequence, condition, and repetition.** Once you can name them in everyday tasks — a recipe, a password rule, an inbox sweep — you have the skeleton of every program. The week stays in plain English; no code yet.

## What you will be able to do by the end of the week

- Name and recognise *sequence*, *condition* and *repetition* in an everyday process.
- Write a 6–10 step sequence with the order made explicit by numbering.
- Express an everyday rule as `if … then … else …` in plain English.
- Draw a small decision tree for a familiar rule.
- Express a repeated action with an explicit stop (`repeat until …`, `for each …`).
- Combine all three into one short process description.

## Must-do minimum vs. deepening

**Must-do minimum — every student should leave Week 5 able to:**
- Name the three building blocks and recognise each in an everyday process.
- Express one condition as `if … then … else …`, anchored in a real-life rule.
- Express one repetition with an explicit, observable stop.
- Combine all three (3–6 sequence steps + at least one condition + at least one repetition) into one process description. **This is the AT2 feeder artefact.**

**Deepening (optional):**
- Spot compound conditions (AND, OR) in rules you have seen.
- Notice a `repeat until` that could run forever; add a safety stop.
- Apply the three blocks to a vocational scene (password rule, ticket routing, inbox sweep).
- Use an LLM as a sparring partner against your decision tree.

## How the three lessons fit together

**Lesson 13** names the *default*: the follower walks the steps top to bottom — sequence. **Lesson 14** names *the test that splits the path*: a condition. **Lesson 15** names *the test that loops the path*: a repetition with a clear stop. Default, split, loop — every process is some weave of those three.

## How this week feeds AT2

**AT2 lands at the end of Week 5 / during Week 6.** It asks you to describe a process with one sequence backbone, one condition, and one repetition. The must-do minimum task is that artefact at a smaller scale. Keep your notes — they are the raw material for AT2.
